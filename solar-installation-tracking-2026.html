Vikram runs a 15-person solar installation company in Nagpur. The Spreadsheet Problem Every Growing Solar Company Hits

The spreadsheet problem in solar is not unique to India — but it is particularly acute here because of how Indian solar businesses are structured. Most companies operate with a distributed field team spread across multiple districts, a small back-office team handling documentation and subsidy applications, and a founder or manager who is simultaneously the senior salesperson, operations head, and chief problem-solver. Everyone is working at full capacity. Nobody has time to maintain a spreadsheet with the discipline that would make it actually useful.

The specific failure modes are well documented by companies that have made the transition away from spreadsheets. First, data freshness: a spreadsheet entry that was accurate at 9 AM is already outdated by 11 AM if a field engineer completed a task and did not update it. Second, version control: when three people are editing the same sheet, conflicts and overwrites corrupt the data. Third, actionability: a spreadsheet shows you that a job is "in progress" — it does not tell you that the job has been in progress for 11 days when it should take 4, or that the delay is because the inverter is stuck in a DTDC depot in Pune.

The fourth and most insidious failure is the invisible information problem. A spreadsheet only knows what someone chose to put in it. It cannot capture the photo the field engineer took of the completed mounting structure. It cannot flag that this particular customer called twice this week asking for updates. It cannot alert you that the DISCOM approval for job number 14 expired because nobody renewed it in time. The things that fall through the cracks are always the things your spreadsheet was not designed to catch.

What End-to-End Installation Tracking Actually Means

True end-to-end installation tracking is not just about knowing which stage a job is at. It is about creating a complete, real-time, role-appropriate view of every job's status — accessible to every relevant team member, updated automatically wherever possible, and flagging exceptions the moment they occur without requiring anyone to go looking for them.

In practice, this means a centralised dashboard where the owner can see the full portfolio of live jobs in a single glance. It means a mobile view for field engineers that shows their jobs for the day, the specific tasks they need to complete at each site, and a simple way to mark tasks done and upload evidence from their phone. It means automated alerts when a job crosses a time threshold — if a site survey has been sitting without a follow-up for 5 days, the system flags it. And it means every team member — sales, technical, documentation, accounts — seeing only the information relevant to their role, without being overwhelmed by data that is not their responsibility.

A solar installation in India is not a single event — it is a multi-week sequence of handoffs between different people and external agencies. Every solar project management system needs to track all 8 of these stages, with clear ownership, expected timelines, and completion criteria for each.

1

Lead Won & Site Survey

The job enters the system the moment the customer signs the agreement. The site survey data — roof dimensions, shading analysis, phase supply, meter location, photos — is captured digitally in the CRM during or immediately after the visit. Owner: Sales Executive. Target completion: Same day as agreement.

2

DISCOM Application Submission

The documentation specialist uses the site survey data to register the customer on the PM Surya Ghar portal and submit the DISCOM application. All documents are collected, verified, and uploaded. Owner: Documentation Team. Target completion: Within 2 working days of agreement.

3

DISCOM Feasibility Approval

Waiting for the DISCOM to approve the rooftop solar application. This is an external dependency — the CRM tracks days elapsed and auto-alerts the team if approval is taking longer than the state average. Owner: Documentation Team (chasing). Target: 7–30 days depending on state.

4

Material Procurement & Delivery

Once DISCOM approval is received, the procurement team orders and schedules delivery of panels, inverter, mounting structure, cables, and switchgear. The CRM tracks purchase order status, expected delivery date, and delivery confirmation with photo. Owner: Procurement / Store. Target: Delivery within 5 working days of DISCOM approval.

5

Installation

The field engineering team carries out the installation. Each sub-task — mounting structure, panel installation, wiring, inverter commissioning, earthing, AC connection — is checked off in the mobile app with photos at each milestone. Owner: Field Engineer. Target: 1–3 days depending on system size.

6

DISCOM Technical Inspection

Installation completion is notified to the DISCOM through the portal. The CRM tracks the inspection request date, scheduled inspection date, and outcome. If inspection fails, the reason is logged and the rectification task is created automatically. Owner: Documentation Team. Target: Inspection within 7–21 days of notification.

7

Net Meter Installation

After passing inspection, the DISCOM schedules net meter installation. This is the most variable stage — the CRM tracks days since inspection clearance and escalates if the DISCOM exceeds the state-standard wait time. Owner: Documentation Team (liaison). Target: 2–8 weeks depending on DISCOM.

8

Commissioning, Handover & AMC Activation

System goes live. Digital handover document is signed by the customer on the field engineer's phone. Final invoice is generated automatically. AMC contract is activated in the CRM and the next scheduled maintenance visit is created. The job moves from Active Installations to Active Customers. Owner: Field Engineer + Accounts. Target: Same day as net meter installation.

Who Sees What: Role-Based Dashboard Views

One of the biggest practical problems with shared spreadsheets is information overload. When everyone sees everything, people stop looking at anything. A project tracking CRM solves this by giving each role a tailored dashboard view — relevant, actionable, and free of noise.

Owner / Manager

Full portfolio overview: total active jobs by stage, jobs behind schedule flagged in red, monthly installations completed vs target, outstanding revenue by project, and team utilisation. The "morning coffee view" — a complete picture in 60 seconds.

Field Engineer

Today's job list with addresses, customer contacts, and specific task checklists per job. Photo upload prompts for each installation milestone. GPS check-in. No pipeline data, no financial data — just the operational tasks for the day.

Sales Executive

Their own leads and active jobs. Pipeline stage for each customer, outstanding follow-up tasks, and proposal status. Visibility into their jobs' installation progress so they can proactively update their customers — but no access to other reps' portfolios.

Documentation Specialist

All jobs requiring DISCOM application submission, pending approvals, inspection requests, and net meter follow-ups. Deadline alerts for DISCOM portal submissions. Subsidy application status by job. Purely the documentation workflow — no technical or financial data.

Real-Time Field Updates: No More Status Calls

The most time-consuming operational habit in most solar companies is the status call — the manager calling the field engineer to ask "where are you, what's happening, is it done?" These calls typically consume 30–45 minutes of both parties' productive time every day, and they produce information that is already obsolete by the time the next call happens.

A mobile-first project tracking CRM eliminates the status call entirely. When a field engineer marks a task complete in the app, the manager's dashboard updates in real time. When photos are uploaded after panel installation, they are visible to the back office within seconds. When GPS check-in confirms the engineer has arrived at a job site, the system logs it automatically without any input from the engineer.

The manager's role shifts from information-chaser to exception-handler. Instead of spending 45 minutes a day tracking down status updates, they spend 10 minutes reviewing the dashboard for flagged exceptions — the one job that is running 3 days behind schedule, the one customer whose inspection has been pending for 18 days, the one field engineer whose check-ins suggest they are spending too long at each site. This is a fundamentally more productive use of management time, and it compounds over weeks and months into a measurably better-run operation.

Automated Customer Communication at Every Milestone

One of the most underappreciated benefits of a project tracking system is what it enables for customer communication. When the system knows exactly where each job is in the pipeline, it can automatically send status update messages to customers at every meaningful milestone — without anyone on your team having to write or send them manually.

This matters enormously for customer satisfaction. The number one complaint from residential solar customers in India is not about product quality or installation quality — it is about not knowing what is happening with their installation. A customer who received clear, timely updates at every stage almost never escalates, almost never calls to complain, and almost always refers your company to their neighbours. A customer who has been waiting three weeks without any update becomes anxious, then frustrated, then a source of negative word-of-mouth — even if their system is installed perfectly on time.

Spotting Bottlenecks Before They Become Delays

The most powerful feature of a well-configured installation tracking system is not real-time visibility — it is predictive visibility. By tracking how long each job spends at each stage, the system builds a baseline of what "normal" looks like for your specific operations in your specific geography. Any job that deviates significantly from that baseline triggers an automated flag before the delay becomes a crisis.

In practice, this means the system can tell you on Day 8 that DISCOM approval for a job in Jaipur is taking longer than usual — before the customer has noticed, before the installation crew has had to reschedule, and before the delay cascades into a procurement problem. That early flag gives the documentation team time to follow up proactively with the DISCOM, often resolving the issue before it affects the customer experience at all.

At the portfolio level, this bottleneck data is invaluable for operational planning. If the tracking data shows that 70% of your delays are concentrated at the DISCOM inspection stage in a particular district, that is a specific, actionable finding — it tells you to prioritise building relationships with the DISCOM inspection team in that district, or to adjust your customer communication to set more realistic expectations for jobs in that area. No spreadsheet can surface this kind of systemic insight automatically.

From Installation to AMC: Tracking the Full Customer Lifecycle

The job does not end when the system goes live. For a solar installation business, every completed installation is the beginning of a long-term customer relationship — and the companies that manage this relationship well generate a significant portion of their revenue from AMC contracts, panel cleaning services, inverter warranties, and referrals. Tracking only the installation phase means leaving the most profitable part of the customer lifecycle unmanaged.

A complete solar project management CRM moves every completed installation into an Active Customer record the moment the job is handed over. The AMC start date, contract terms, and next scheduled maintenance visit are all logged automatically. When a maintenance visit is due, the system creates a task for the field engineer, sends a reminder to the customer, and tracks the visit completion with photos and sign-off just like an installation job. When the AMC comes up for renewal, an automated message goes to the customer 30 days before the renewal date — giving your team ample time to collect the renewal payment without any manual follow-up.

Solar companies that implement this full-lifecycle tracking consistently report AMC renewal rates above 80% — compared to an industry average of 40–50% for companies managing renewals manually. The difference is simply that automated, timely reminders work far better than sporadic manual follow-ups, and customers who feel well-served throughout the AMC period are far more likely to renew.

Migrating From Excel to a CRM: How Long It Really Takes

The most common reason solar companies delay moving away from spreadsheets is the perceived pain of migration. The reality is far less daunting than most owners expect — particularly with a solar-specific CRM built for this exact transition.

For a company with up to 50 active jobs and 200 customer records, the full migration typically takes one working day: export your existing customer and job data from Excel as a CSV, import it into the CRM using the guided importer, configure your pipeline stages to match your existing process, and install the mobile app on every team member's phone. Solar CRM's onboarding support team handles the import and configuration — you do not need a technical person on your staff to complete it.

The first week after migration is the transition week — some team members will revert to WhatsApp habits while they adjust to the new system. The manager's job during this week is simple: every time someone sends a job update via WhatsApp, acknowledge it and ask them to also log it in the CRM. By the end of week two, most teams are operating natively in the CRM and the WhatsApp group has become what it should always have been: a tool for urgent communication, not a project management system.

⚠️
Do Not Try to Replicate Your Spreadsheet in the CRM The most common migration mistake is trying to recreate every column and tab of your existing spreadsheet inside the CRM. This defeats the purpose. When you migrate, simplify — define 6–8 clear stage names that represent your actual process, and let the CRM's automation replace the manual tracking you were doing in Excel. Complexity is the enemy of adoption.
