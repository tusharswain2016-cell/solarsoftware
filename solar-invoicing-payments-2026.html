Ankit runs a solar installation company in Jaipur. Last month his team completed 18 residential installations — a record month. On paper, he billed ₹54 lakhs. In his bank account? ₹19 lakhs. The rest was sitting in overdue invoices spread across 11 different clients in varying stages of "I'll transfer it this week."

Ankit is not unusual. Walk into almost any solar installation business in India and you will find the same pattern: excellent at installing solar, terrible at collecting money. Proposals go out on time. Installations happen on schedule. And then the billing process falls off a cliff — manual invoices in Tally, payment reminders via WhatsApp that feel awkward to send, and a growing pile of receivables that quietly strangle the company's ability to buy materials for the next batch of jobs.

In 2026, this is entirely solvable. Not through hiring a dedicated collections person, not through aggressive legal notices, and not through uncomfortable follow-up calls. The solution is a combination of smart invoicing automation, structured payment terms, and a systematic reminder sequence that collects money consistently without a single human having to ask for it.

The Cash Flow Crisis Hiding in Your Receivables

Late payments in solar are not just an inconvenience — they create a compounding operational problem that most business owners underestimate until it is serious. When a client delays payment by 30 days on a ₹3 lakh installation, you are effectively funding that customer's solar system with your own working capital for a month. If you have six such clients at any given time, you are carrying ₹18 lakhs in unpaid work — money you have already spent on panels, inverters, mounting structures, and labour.

This is money you cannot use to pay your supplier for the next batch of panels, which means you either delay the next job, take a short-term loan at high interest, or dip into personal savings. Solar companies in India that grow fast and fail fast almost always fail for the same reason: they scaled installations faster than they scaled collections, and the working capital gap became impossible to bridge.

The good news is that unlike lead generation or technical quality, cash flow improvement is largely a process problem — and process problems are fixable with the right systems.

Why Solar Clients Pay Late — and It's Not What You Think

The instinctive explanation for late payment is that clients are unwilling to pay — that they are difficult, or that they are intentionally stretching your terms. In the vast majority of cases, this is simply not true. Most residential solar customers are genuinely happy with their installation. They intend to pay. They are just not paying because nobody has made it easy, urgent, or frictionless to do so.

The real reasons solar clients pay late in India are almost always structural. The invoice arrived 10 days after installation because the company was busy with the next job. The invoice was a PDF in an email that the customer meant to forward to their CA but forgot. The payment terms said "15 days" but there was no reminder, so the customer treated it as optional. The only payment method mentioned was bank transfer, and the customer kept meaning to do it but never got around to looking up the account details. There was no consequence — no reminder, no follow-up, nothing — so there was no urgency.

Fix these structural issues and collections improve dramatically — without any confrontation, without legal notices, and without damaging a single customer relationship.

Automated GST Invoicing: Generate & Send in 60 Seconds

The first structural fix is eliminating the gap between job completion and invoice delivery. In most solar companies, invoicing is a manual process that happens days or weeks after installation — when someone finds time to open Tally, enter the details, generate a PDF, and email it. Every day of delay is a day of extra float given to the customer.

A solar CRM with integrated GST billing eliminates this gap entirely. The moment the field engineer marks a job as complete in the mobile app — tapping "Installation Done" on the rooftop — the system can automatically generate a fully compliant GST invoice using the job's pre-entered details: customer name, address, GSTIN, system specifications, amount, and applicable GST rate. The invoice is sent to the customer's WhatsApp and email within seconds of job completion, while the installation crew is still packing up their tools.

GST-Compliant PDF

Instant WhatsApp Delivery

Due Date Auto-Set

Live Payment Status

Structuring Milestone-Based Payments the Right Way

For residential solar installations, collecting the full amount in one payment after installation creates maximum risk for your cash flow and maximum friction for the customer. The industry best practice — and the structure used by every high-performing solar company in India — is a milestone-based payment schedule tied to project progress events, not arbitrary dates.

Milestone % of Total Trigger Event Typical Amount (5 kWp)
Booking Advance 20–30% Agreement signed ₹30,000–₹45,000
Material Delivery 40–50% Panels & inverter delivered to site ₹60,000–₹75,000
Installation Complete 20–25% System commissioned & functional ₹30,000–₹37,500
Final Handover 5–10% DISCOM approval & net meter installed ₹7,500–₹15,000

This structure protects you in three important ways. First, you are never more than one milestone's worth of money at risk if a customer delays. Second, the customer never faces a large, sudden payment demand — each milestone is a smaller, more psychologically manageable amount. Third, tying payment to visible project progress events gives the customer a clear, fair reason to pay: they can see what they are paying for at each stage.

The solar CRM should be configured to automatically generate and send each milestone invoice the moment the corresponding trigger event is logged — delivery confirmed, installation marked complete, DISCOM approval received. This removes the human dependency from the billing process entirely.

The Smart Payment Reminder Sequence

The most powerful single change a solar company can make to its collections process is implementing a structured, automated payment reminder sequence. Not a manual reminder that someone sends when they remember. Not a single reminder on the due date. A pre-programmed, multi-touchpoint sequence that runs automatically for every invoice, every time, with zero human effort.

Here is the exact sequence used by the highest-performing solar companies in the Solar CRM network:

Day 0 — Invoice Sent
Invoice + UPI Payment Link
Invoice PDF delivered via WhatsApp and email with an embedded UPI payment link. Message tone: warm and professional — "Your Solar CRM invoice for ₹37,500 is ready. Pay instantly via UPI: [link]"
Day 3 — Early Reminder
Friendly Nudge via WhatsApp
A gentle reminder 3 days after invoice. Tone: helpful, not pressuring. "Just a reminder that your invoice of ₹37,500 is due on [date]. Tap here to pay via UPI in 30 seconds: [link]". Includes the invoice PDF again for easy reference.
Day 7 — Due Date Reminder
Due Date Alert with Urgency
Sent on the due date itself. Tone shifts to clear urgency without being aggressive. "Your payment of ₹37,500 is due today. Pay now to keep your solar warranty active and avoid service delays: [UPI link]." Tying payment to a benefit (warranty, service continuity) is far more effective than generic reminders.
Day 10 — First Overdue Notice
Overdue Alert — Automated
Three days after the due date if still unpaid. Professional and factual. "Your invoice #INV-2026-0412 for ₹37,500 is now 3 days overdue. Please arrange payment today or contact us to discuss a payment plan." This message also triggers an internal alert to the account manager to make a personal call.
Day 17 — Escalation
Senior Follow-Up + Escalation Flag
10 days overdue. The CRM flags this account in the owner/manager dashboard. A more direct message goes out, and the account manager is assigned a task to visit the client in person or schedule a call. At this stage, the human element is reintroduced — the automated sequence has done its work, and personal contact is now appropriate.

This sequence recovers the vast majority of outstanding payments on its own — without a single manual follow-up from your team. Solar companies using this automated sequence typically see their average collection period drop from 28 days to 13 days within the first two months.

UPI Payment Links: Removing All Friction from Collection

One of the most underutilised improvements available to solar companies in India right now is the embedded UPI payment link. When a customer receives an invoice, the single biggest barrier to immediate payment is not willingness — it is friction. Finding your bank account number, opening their banking app, navigating to NEFT/RTGS, and entering all the details takes 4–6 minutes on a good day. For most people, that is 4–6 minutes they do not have when the invoice arrives during a workday.

A UPI payment link reduces that friction to approximately 15 seconds: tap the link, confirm the amount in their UPI app, enter their PIN, done. When every invoice includes a UPI payment link directly in the WhatsApp message body, payment rates within the first 48 hours jump significantly — typically from around 18% to over 45% for residential solar customers.

UPI Integration Best Practices for Solar Companies

  • Generate a unique UPI deep-link for every invoice — pre-filled with the exact amount and your VPA so the customer cannot accidentally pay the wrong amount
  • Include the UPI link in both the WhatsApp message body AND inside the PDF invoice as a QR code — capturing both mobile and desktop payment preferences
  • Configure the CRM to automatically mark an invoice as paid the moment UPI confirmation is received — no manual reconciliation needed
  • For commercial clients and large amounts over ₹1 lakh, also provide NEFT/RTGS details — UPI has transaction limits that may not cover full commercial invoices
  • Test your payment links quarterly to ensure your bank's UPI integration is functioning correctly — a broken link silently kills collections

Handling Genuinely Overdue Invoices Without Damaging Relationships

Even with the best automated systems, some invoices will go genuinely overdue — beyond 15–20 days past due date. These require human intervention, but the approach matters enormously. The solar industry in India is heavily referral-driven. A homeowner in Pune who has a bad experience with your collections process will not refer your company to their neighbour, their housing society, or their relatives. The goal is to collect the money while preserving — or even strengthening — the relationship.

❌ What Not to Do

Sending WhatsApp messages with phrases like "your payment is still pending, please do the needful" every two days. Calling multiple times without getting through and leaving no message. Sending a legal notice on day 20. Threatening to remove the solar system. All of these destroy goodwill and generate referral-killing resentment — even when you are completely in the right.

✅ What Actually Works

One personal call from the account manager or owner with a tone of genuine concern: "Hi Mr Sharma, I noticed your invoice is still outstanding — just wanted to make sure there isn't a problem with the installation or paperwork our side. We are happy to set up a brief call if anything needs clarifying." This reframes the conversation from collection to customer service, often unlocks the real reason for delay (lost invoice, wrong amount, waiting for CA), and resolves the issue within 24 hours.

The other powerful tool for genuinely stuck receivables is the payment plan offer. For customers who are genuinely cash-constrained, offering to split a ₹50,000 overdue amount into two payments of ₹25,000 — one immediate and one in 15 days — is almost always better than waiting another 45 days for the full amount. A partial payment today is better for your cash flow than a theoretical full payment next month, and the offer of flexibility builds goodwill that turns into referrals.

The Collections Dashboard Every Solar Owner Needs

You cannot manage what you cannot see. Most solar company owners have a vague, anxiety-inducing sense that there is money owed to them — but no clear picture of exactly how much, by whom, for how long, and what stage of the reminder sequence each invoice is at. This vagueness is itself a problem: it leads to either over-chasing (which damages relationships) or under-chasing (which delays collection).

A solar CRM's receivables dashboard gives you complete clarity at a glance. Every outstanding invoice is visible, sortable by age, amount, and customer. The dashboard shows the total outstanding amount, the amount that is current (within terms), the amount that is 1–14 days overdue, and the amount that is 15+ days overdue. Colour coding makes the picture immediately readable — green for current, amber for mildly overdue, red for seriously overdue.

More importantly, the dashboard shows the automated reminder status for each invoice: which reminders have been sent, which have been delivered and read (via WhatsApp read receipts), and which accounts have been flagged for manual follow-up. The account manager can see at 8 AM every morning exactly which three or four accounts need a personal touch that day — and focus their energy precisely where it is needed rather than reviewing every outstanding invoice manually.

Your 30-Day Cash Flow Improvement Plan

The changes described in this article are not theoretical — they are implementable in the next 30 days by any solar company in India with 5 or more active projects per month. Here is a practical sequenced plan:

1

Days 1–3: Audit Your Current Receivables

Before fixing the future, get complete clarity on the present. List every outstanding invoice: client name, amount, invoice date, due date, and days overdue. Calculate your total DSO. This number is your baseline — you will be measuring against it in 30 days. Most solar owners who do this for the first time are surprised by how large the number is.

2

Days 4–7: Fix Your Payment Terms and Milestone Structure

Update your standard agreement template to include the 4-milestone payment schedule. Brief your sales team on how to present payment terms during the proposal stage — not at the end as an afterthought, but upfront as a structured, professional plan. Customers who understand and agree to milestone payments before the job starts pay significantly more reliably than those who receive a single invoice at the end.

3

Days 8–14: Configure Automated Invoicing in Your CRM

Set up your GST invoice templates in Solar CRM. Link each milestone trigger event to an automatic invoice generation action. Configure your UPI payment link integration. Test the full flow on one live job — from completion to invoice delivery to payment link. Verify that read receipts are coming through on WhatsApp and that UPI payments are auto-reconciling in the dashboard.

4

Days 15–21: Activate the Reminder Sequence

Build and activate the 5-touchpoint automated reminder sequence. Set the escalation alert threshold (typically 10 days overdue) to flag accounts for manual follow-up. Run the sequence for all new invoices generated from this point forward. Separately, personally call your current top 5 overdue accounts using the relationship-first script — do not send another automated message for these.

5

Days 22–30: Review, Measure, and Optimise

Check your DSO against the baseline. Review which reminder touchpoints are generating the most immediate payments (for most companies it is the Day 3 reminder with the UPI link). Check the dashboard for any invoices that have slipped through the automated sequence without payment — these get personal calls. By day 30, your average collection period should already have dropped by at least 7–10 days.
