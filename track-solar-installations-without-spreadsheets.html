Ask any solar operations manager in India to open their project tracker right now, and here is what you will typically find: a Google Sheet with 200+ rows, last updated by someone three days ago, with columns for "Survey Done?", "DISCOM Approval Status", "Installation Date (Approx)", and a "Notes" column that contains a mix of WhatsApp messages, phone call summaries, and cryptic abbreviations that only one person understands.

Somewhere in that sheet is a customer who was promised an installation last week and has called three times for an update. Somewhere else is a DISCOM application that stalled because nobody followed up. And there are at least two customers whose subsidy disbursement is overdue because nobody updated the commissioning date on the portal.

This is not a discipline problem or a staffing problem. It is a systems problem — and it becomes a business-limiting problem the moment you try to scale from 30 installations a month to 80. This guide explains what proper end-to-end installation tracking looks like, why spreadsheets fundamentally cannot deliver it, and how to move to a system that keeps every project moving without manual chasing.

The Spreadsheet Problem: Why It Gets Worse as You Grow

Spreadsheets are not bad tools. For a solar company doing 10–15 installations a month, a well-structured Google Sheet can work reasonably well. The problem is that spreadsheets scale linearly with complexity — every new installation, every new team member, every new stage you need to track adds friction. At some point, the spreadsheet stops being a tool and starts being a liability.

The specific failure modes of spreadsheet-based tracking in solar operations are well-documented by companies that have moved away from them. Here are the four that cause the most damage:

No single source of truth. Field engineers update WhatsApp. Office staff update the sheet. The manager checks both. They never quite match.
No proactive alerts. A row sitting at "DISCOM Pending — 3 weeks" never shouts for attention. Follow-ups only happen when someone remembers to look.
Documents live elsewhere. The Aadhaar copy is on someone's phone. The signed agreement is on Google Drive in a folder no one can find. The electricity bill was printed and filed.
No customer-facing visibility. The customer has no way to know their project status except by calling you — and every call is time your team cannot spend on installations.
⚠️
The Scaling Cliff Solar companies using spreadsheets reliably hit a wall between 40–60 active projects. Below that threshold, a smart operations person can hold the context in their head. Above it, projects start slipping through — DISCOM follow-ups missed, subsidy portals not updated, customers not notified. The cliff is not visible until you are already falling off it.

What End-to-End Installation Tracking Actually Means

End-to-end tracking means that for every installation in your pipeline, you can answer these questions in under 30 seconds without making a single phone call:

  • What stage is this project at right now?
  • Who is the assigned field engineer and what is their current workload?
  • What documents have been collected, and which are still missing?
  • Has the DISCOM application been submitted, and what is its current status?
  • When was the customer last contacted and what was the outcome?
  • Is anything blocking this project from moving to the next stage?
  • What is the next scheduled action and when?

A spreadsheet cannot answer all of these questions reliably because it is a static data store — it only reflects what someone typed into it, and only as recently as the last time someone updated it. A purpose-built CRM answers all of them in real time because every action — document upload, stage change, WhatsApp message sent, site visit logged — automatically updates the central record.

The 12 Stages Every Solar Installation Should Move Through

The most important structural decision in your tracking system is defining your stages precisely. Vague stages like "In Progress" or "Pending" are useless — they tell you where a project is parked, not what needs to happen next. The stages below are used by high-performing solar CRM users across India and are tuned specifically for the PM Surya Ghar process:

1

Lead Qualified

The customer has been confirmed as eligible: residential owner-occupier, active DISCOM connection, no prior central subsidy. Basic system size scoped from electricity bill review. Site visit scheduled.

2

Site Survey Completed

Field engineer has visited the site, assessed roof condition and orientation, confirmed shading analysis, photographed the installation area, and noted any structural or electrical concerns. Survey report uploaded to CRM on-site via mobile app.

3

Proposal Sent & Agreement Signed

Detailed system design proposal shared with customer. Signed installation agreement collected (digitally or physically) and uploaded. Customer briefed on PM Surya Ghar process, timelines, and the fact that subsidy arrives after commissioning.

4

Documents Collected

All KYC and subsidy documents collected: Aadhaar, PAN, bank passbook, property proof, electricity bill, passport photo. Every document uploaded to the project record in CRM. Document completeness checklist marked 100% before proceeding.

5

Portal Registration & DISCOM Application Submitted

Customer registered on pmsuryaghar.gov.in. DISCOM feasibility application submitted with all required documents. Application reference number logged in CRM. Automated reminder set for DISCOM follow-up in 10 days.

6

DISCOM Feasibility Approved

DISCOM has granted feasibility approval — confirmed via portal. Approval date and reference logged. Installation scheduling triggered. Customer notified automatically via WhatsApp message.

7

Payment Received / Financing Confirmed

Customer advance or full payment received and recorded. If financing, loan sanction letter uploaded and disbursement schedule noted. Invoice generated from CRM and sent to customer.

8

Installation In Progress

Panels, inverter, and mounting structure being installed. Field engineer logs daily progress photos via mobile CRM. Equipment serial numbers recorded. Estimated completion date visible to operations manager on dashboard.

9

Installation Completed — Portal Updated

Physical installation complete. Completion photos (timestamped) uploaded to CRM. National portal updated by operations team to trigger DISCOM inspection scheduling. Customer notified of completion.

10

DISCOM Inspection Passed

DISCOM technical officer has inspected the installation and confirmed compliance. Inspection date, officer name, and any snag items logged. If snags raised, remediation tracked as a sub-task before this stage is marked complete.

11

Net Meter Installed & System Commissioned

DISCOM has installed the bidirectional net meter. System is live and generating power. Commissioning certificate received and uploaded. Final balance invoice issued. Customer notified that subsidy disbursement is now in process.

12

Subsidy Disbursed — Project Closed

Customer confirms subsidy of ₹30,000–₹78,000 credited to their bank account. Project marked complete. Referral request sent automatically. Warranty documentation shared. Project archived with full audit trail.

Field Operations: Capturing What Happens on Site

The biggest gap in most solar tracking systems is the last mile: what actually happens during the site survey and installation. Field engineers are not desk workers. They are on rooftops in 40-degree heat, dealing with structural surprises, equipment deliveries, and customers who have changed their minds about panel placement. Asking them to come back to the office and update a spreadsheet at the end of the day is a recipe for lost information.

The right solution is a mobile CRM that field engineers can update from their phones while they are still on site — or even while still on the roof. The workflow for a site survey should take under five minutes on a mobile device: open the job, tap through the site checklist, photograph the roof and electrical panel, note any issues in a voice memo or text field, and hit submit. The CRM instantly updates the project record, notifies the operations manager, and logs the timestamp and GPS location.

What a Field Engineer Should Capture On-Site — In the CRM, Not on Paper

  • Site survey checklist: Roof type, condition, area available, shading obstacles, compass orientation, access constraints. Tick boxes rather than free text to ensure consistency across engineers.
  • Timestamped geo-tagged photos: Roof overview, electrical meter location, proposed panel layout, any structural concerns. Photos attached directly to the project record — not sent via WhatsApp.
  • Equipment serial numbers: Every panel and inverter serial number photographed or scanned during installation. This is essential for warranty claims and technical audits.
  • Installation sign-off photo: Final completed installation photograph with clear visibility of all panels and the inverter, logged with timestamp as proof of completion date.
  • Customer sign-off: Digital signature or photograph of the customer acknowledging installation completion — used to trigger the final balance invoice and portal update.

Tracking DISCOM Approvals Across Multiple States

For solar companies operating across multiple states — or even multiple districts within a single state — DISCOM approval tracking is the single most complex operational challenge. Each DISCOM has different portal interfaces, different processing timelines, different inspection procedures, and different escalation contacts. Managing this manually for 50+ active applications simultaneously is nearly impossible.

Feasibility Approved Approval date, approved capacity, any conditions noted Notify sales + ops immediately N/A — completed stage

Inspection Completed Date, officer name, outcome, any snags raised Trigger snag remediation task if failed Re-inspection request within 5 days of snag fix
Net Meter Applied Application date, expected installation window Remind team every 2 weeks until installed Customer-facing update + DISCOM follow-up

The key insight here is that DISCOM tracking should be proactive, not reactive. Your CRM should alert your operations team when an application is approaching the upper end of the normal processing window — before the customer calls you to complain. The difference between a solar company that manages 100 active DISCOM applications and one that cannot manage 30 is almost entirely a question of whether these alerts exist and are acted on.

Automated Customer Communication at Every Milestone

The most common complaint solar customers have — more common than technical issues, more common than cost surprises — is not knowing what is happening with their project. A PM Surya Ghar installation takes 8–20 weeks from start to subsidy disbursement. That is a long time to feel like you are waiting with no information.

The best solar companies have solved this entirely with automated milestone messages sent via WhatsApp in the customer's preferred language. Every time a project stage advances in the CRM, a pre-written message goes out automatically. No manual sending. No remembering. The customer feels informed, and your team's phone stops ringing with status enquiries.

The 8 WhatsApp Messages Every Solar Customer Should Receive Automatically

  • Survey Scheduled: "Namaste [Name], our engineer [Engineer Name] will visit your home on [Date] at [Time] for the site survey. Please ensure roof access is available."
  • DISCOM Application Submitted: "Your PM Surya Ghar application has been submitted to [DISCOM Name]. Reference: [Ref No.]. Approval typically takes [X–Y] days. We will update you as soon as it is approved."
  • DISCOM Approved: "Great news, [Name]! Your DISCOM feasibility has been approved. We will now schedule your installation — our team will contact you within 2 working days."
  • Installation Scheduled: "Your solar installation is confirmed for [Date]. Our team of [N] engineers will arrive by [Time]. Please keep the roof and electrical panel area accessible."
  • Installation Completed: "Installation complete! Your [X] kWp system is ready. We have updated the government portal — the DISCOM inspection will be scheduled in the coming days."
  • Inspection Passed: "Your installation has passed the DISCOM technical inspection. Net meter installation is now being processed — this is the final step before subsidy disbursement."
  • System Commissioned: "Your solar system is now officially commissioned and generating clean energy! Your subsidy of ₹[Amount] will be credited to your bank account within 30 days."
  • Subsidy Disbursed: "Excellent news! Your PM Surya Ghar subsidy of ₹[Amount] has been credited to your bank account. Thank you for going solar with us — could you share your experience with your neighbours?"

The Manager Dashboard: Real-Time Visibility Across All Projects

A solar operations manager running 60–100 active projects needs a dashboard that shows the complete state of the business in a single view — without opening individual project records or reading through status update messages. The right dashboard answers the questions that actually matter for running the business effectively.

The most valuable views for a solar operations manager are: a stage funnel showing how many projects are at each of the 12 installation stages; a stalled projects list showing any project that has not moved stage in more than 7 days (colour-coded red after 14 days); a DISCOM pending queue organised by state with days-since-submission visible; an upcoming installations calendar showing confirmed installation dates and engineer assignments for the next 14 days; and a subsidy disbursement tracker showing outstanding subsidy amounts and estimated credit dates for all commissioned projects.

Migrating From Spreadsheets: A Practical Transition Plan

The biggest fear solar companies have about moving away from spreadsheets is the migration itself — the idea that it will take weeks, require a technical consultant, and disrupt operations during the transition. In practice, a well-planned migration to a solar CRM takes 3–5 working days and can be done while operations continue in parallel.

1

Day 1: Export and Clean Your Existing Data

Export your current spreadsheet to CSV. Clean up duplicates and inconsistencies — standardise customer names, phone numbers (with country code), and project stages against your new 12-stage pipeline. This cleaning process typically surfaces 10–15% of records that were duplicated or mislabelled in the old system.

2

Day 2: Configure Your Pipeline and Custom Fields

Set up your 12-stage pipeline in Solar CRM. Add any custom fields specific to your operations: DISCOM reference number, system size (kWp), panel brand, inverter model, engineer assigned. Configure role-based access so field engineers, sales staff, and accounts see the fields relevant to them.

3

Day 3: Import Existing Projects and Assign Stages

Import your cleaned CSV into Solar CRM. For each active project, verify the stage assignment — this is the most time-consuming part, but it is also the moment when you will discover projects that were misclassified or stuck in the old system. Many companies find 5–10 projects they had lost track of entirely.

4

Day 4: Train Your Team — Sales, Field, Operations

Run a 60-minute training session for each team group: sales on lead management and proposal tracking; field engineers on the mobile app and site checklist; operations on DISCOM tracking and the manager dashboard. Solar CRM's mobile app is designed to be usable without training — but a structured session reduces resistance and ensures consistent usage from day one.

5

Day 5: Go Live — Spreadsheet as Read-Only Archive

From this day forward, all new updates go into Solar CRM. The old spreadsheet is kept as a read-only archive for historical reference but is no longer updated. Enforce this clearly with the team — the most common migration failure is people continuing to update both systems for "safety," which immediately creates the same confusion the migration was designed to eliminate.

Frequently Asked Questions

We are only doing 20 installations a month — do we really need a CRM?
At 20 installations a month, a spreadsheet can work — but you are almost certainly already losing some efficiency to manual chasing, missed follow-ups, and documents being in the wrong place. The more important question is: do you want to grow? If you plan to scale to 50+ installations a month, the systems you build at 20 will either enable or limit that growth. Companies that start with a CRM at 20 installations a month scale 2–3× faster than those that wait until the pain is unbearable at 60.
My field engineers are not tech-savvy. Will they actually use a mobile CRM?
This is the most common concern, and the data consistently shows it is not the barrier people expect. Field engineers who use smartphones for WhatsApp — which is essentially all of them — can use a well-designed mobile CRM app within a week. The key is making the CRM simpler than the alternative: photographing a document and tapping "upload" in the app is easier than photographing it and sending it to five different WhatsApp groups. The trick is ensuring the app is genuinely mobile-first — not a desktop interface squeezed onto a phone screen.
How do we handle projects that don't follow the standard 12-stage flow?
Every solar installation business has edge cases — inspection failures, customers who need the system upsized after installation, housing society complications, equipment supply delays. A good CRM handles these through sub-tasks, notes, and the ability to move stages non-linearly when needed. The pipeline defines the normal flow; exceptions are handled through task management within the project record. The goal is not rigid stage adherence — it is full visibility into where each project is and what is blocking it.
Can the CRM track projects in multiple states with different DISCOMs?
Yes — and this is where a CRM specifically built for Indian solar operations delivers the most value. The manager dashboard can filter all projects by state, by DISCOM, by district, or by assigned engineer, giving you a state-specific view of your pipeline at any time. DISCOM-specific approval timelines can be configured as benchmarks so the auto-alert thresholds are appropriate for each state: 14 days for Gujarat, 30 days for UP.
What happens to our historical data when we migrate?
Your historical data — completed projects, old customer records, past invoices — should be imported into the CRM during migration and marked as completed or archived. This gives you searchable records for warranty claims, referral tracking, and customer reactivation campaigns without cluttering your active pipeline view. Most solar companies find that their historical data is more valuable once it is in a searchable, structured CRM than it ever was sitting in a spreadsheet that nobody opened.
How long before we see a real improvement in project cycle time?
Most solar companies see measurable improvement in project cycle time within the first 60 days after full CRM adoption. The biggest early gains come from document collection — completing this before installation rather than chasing it afterward eliminates the most common source of DISCOM application delays. Teams report saving 3–5 hours per week per operations staff member within the first month, and 10–15% reduction in average days from survey to commissioning within 90 days.
